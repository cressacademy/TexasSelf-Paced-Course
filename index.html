Property Ownership & Legal Framework

Module 1 — The Word for This Module
“In all your ways acknowledge Him, and He shall direct your paths.”
Proverbs 3:6
This module lays the ground of everything the exam asks: what property is, who may own it, and how the law protects it. Build slowly — a settled foundation carries weight later.
Real Property vs. Personal Property▾

Start with the plain idea

Real property is the land and everything permanently attached to it, plus the legal rights that come with owning it. Personal property is everything else — the movable, unattached things. A house is real property. The couch inside it is personal property. The exam loves the gray area between the two, so we settle it with a test.

The MARIA test for fixtures
When something started as personal property but got attached to the land, MARIA tells you whether it became a fixture (real property):
MMethod of attachment
AAdaptability to the property
RRelationship of the parties
IIntention when installed
AAgreement between the parties

Intention is usually the strongest factor a court weighs, but Agreement trumps everything. If the parties wrote down who keeps the item, that written agreement wins over every other factor.

💡
Memory trick. MARIA — and “Agreement Always wins.” The last A beats the other four letters every time.

The bundle of rights

Owning real property means holding a bundle of rights, not just a patch of dirt. Picture five sticks in one bundle: the right to Possess, to Control, to Enjoy, to Exclude, and to Dispose. An owner can hand out one stick (lease the right to possess) while keeping the rest.

Appurtenant rights
Rights that travel with the land — water rights, mineral rights, air rights. “Appurtenant” means “belongs to and runs with” the property, so they pass to the next owner automatically unless carved out.

Transfer follows the property type. Real property transfers by deed. Personal property transfers by a bill of sale. Trade fixtures installed by a commercial tenant stay personal property and can be removed before the lease ends.

Worked example
A tenant bolts custom shelving into the wall of a leased bakery. Is it real or personal property? Run MARIA: it is bolted in (method suggests fixture), but it is trade fixtures installed by a business tenant. Trade fixtures stay personal property and may be removed before the lease ends — as long as the tenant repairs the holes.
🎯
On the exam. Expect at least one MARIA question and one “is this a fixture” scenario. When an answer choice mentions a written agreement about the item, that choice usually wins.
Forms of Co-Ownership▾

Three ways two or more people hold title

When more than one person owns property, the form of co-ownership decides what happens when one owner dies and how the shares work. Three forms dominate the exam.

Tenancy in Common (TIC)
The default when nothing else is stated. No right of survivorship. Shares can be unequal. When an owner dies, that share passes through their will or heirs — not to the other co-owners.
Joint Tenancy (JT)
Requires the four unities of TTIP and carries the right of survivorship — the survivor absorbs the deceased owner's share automatically, outside probate.
TTime — acquired together
TTitle — same document
IInterest — equal shares
PPossession — equal right to whole

All four unities must exist for a joint tenancy. Break any one and the joint tenancy severs, converting that share to a tenancy in common.

💡
Memory trick. TTIP — the four unities of joint tenancy. Miss one and survivorship is gone. “Severance turns JT into TIC.”
Texas Community Property
Texas is a community property state. Property acquired during marriage is community property, owned equally by both spouses. Gifts and inheritance received by one spouse are separate property. Income earned from separate property during marriage becomes community property.
💡
Memory trick. Community property in Texas: “One to buy, two to sell.” One spouse can often purchase, but selling the homestead requires both signatures.
FeatureTenancy in CommonJoint TenancyCommunity Property (TX)
SurvivorshipNoYesNo (passes by will/law)
Equal shares requiredNoYesYes (spouses)
Who can hold itAnyoneAnyoneMarried spouses only
On deathPasses by will/heirsSurvivor absorbs shareDeceased half passes by will/law
Worked example
Three siblings own a ranch as joint tenants. One sells his interest to an outsider. That sale breaks the unities of time and title for his share, severing it. Now the outsider is a tenant in common with the two remaining siblings, who still hold their two-thirds as joint tenants with survivorship between themselves.
🎯
On the exam. Watch for “who gets the share when an owner dies.” TIC → heirs/will. JT → survivor. Community property → the deceased spouse's half passes by will or Texas law.
Estates in Land & Leaseholds▾

Freehold estates — you own it

An estate is the degree, quantity, and duration of your ownership. Freehold estates last an indefinite time and mean actual ownership.

Fee simple absolute
The highest, fullest form of ownership. It lasts forever and has no conditions attached. This is what most buyers receive.
Fee simple defeasible
Ownership with a string attached. A determinable fee ends automatically if a condition is broken (“so long as…”). A fee subject to condition subsequent lets the grantor choose to reclaim it (“on condition that…”).
Life estate
Ownership measured by someone's lifetime. The life tenant may use the property but cannot commit waste (permanent damage that harms the future owner). When the measuring life ends, title passes to the remainderman, or reverts to the grantor (reversion). A life estate pur autre vie is measured by the life of a third person.
💡
Memory trick. “Waste” = a life tenant harming the property's value. The remainderman is the future owner waiting in line.

Leasehold estates — you occupy it

A leasehold gives the right to possess without owning. Four types appear on the exam:

LeaseholdKey trait
Estate for yearsFixed start and end date; ends automatically, no notice
Periodic tenancyRenews period to period (month-to-month); needs notice to end
Estate at willNo fixed term; either party ends it, usually with notice
Estate at sufferanceTenant stays past the lease without permission (holdover)
Worked example
A tenant signs a 12-month lease with clear dates. That is an estate for years — it ends automatically on the last day, no notice required. If the tenant refuses to leave afterward, they become a tenant at sufferance (a holdover), and the landlord can pursue eviction.
🎯
On the exam. Know the difference: estate for years ends by itself; periodic tenancy needs proper notice; sufferance is the wrongful holdover.
Legal Descriptions▾

Three ways to describe land legally

A street address is not a legal description. The exam tests three formal methods that pin down land precisely enough to convey it.

Metes and bounds
Describes a boundary by direction and distance, starting and ending at the Point of Beginning (POB). The description must “close” — return to where it started. Used for irregular parcels.
Lot and block (recorded plat)
References a lot and block number on a subdivision map recorded with the county. The simplest description — common for city homes.
Rectangular (government) survey
Uses principal meridians (north–south lines) and base lines (east–west lines) to build a grid of townships and sections.
UnitSize
Township6 miles × 6 miles (36 square miles)
Section1 square mile = 640 acres
Acre43,560 square feet
💡
Memory trick. 43,560 square feet per acre — remember the “old lady driving I-35 at 60 mph” (43,560). And 640 acres in a section.
Worked example
A parcel is described as “the NE 1/4 of the SE 1/4 of Section 8.” A section is 640 acres. One quarter of a quarter = 640 ÷ 4 ÷ 4 = 40 acres. Fraction problems always multiply the fractions, then multiply by 640.
🎯
On the exam. You will likely see a metes-and-bounds “where does it start” question (answer: Point of Beginning) and an acreage math question using 640 acres and 43,560 sq ft.
Texas Homestead Protection▾

Automatic, constitutional, and strong

Texas homestead protection shields a family's primary residence from most creditors. It is automatic and constitutional — you do not apply for the creditor protection. Do not confuse it with the homestead tax exemption, which does require an application.

Acreage limits
Urban homestead: up to 10 acres. Rural homestead for a family: up to 200 acres. Rural homestead for a single adult: up to 100 acres.
💡
Memory trick. Homestead acreage: 10 urban, 200 rural family, 100 rural single. “Ten in town, two hundred for a family in the country, one hundred single.”
The liens that CAN still reach a homestead
Homestead protection is strong but not absolute. Five debts can still attach: (1) purchase-money mortgage, (2) property taxes (super-priority), (3) mechanic's/construction liens properly signed, (4) HOA assessments, and (5) home-equity loans.
Lien on homesteadRedemption period
Property tax sale2 years
HOA foreclosure180 days
Mortgage foreclosureNone (zero)

Homestead rights end only by sale, death, or abandonment — and cannot be waived by contract. Both spouses must sign to sell or encumber the homestead: “one to buy, two to sell.”

Worked example
A family owns a home on 8 acres inside city limits with a mortgage. A credit-card company wins a judgment and tries to force a sale. Homestead protection blocks it — the 8 acres is within the 10-acre urban limit, and a general judgment creditor is not one of the five liens that can reach a homestead.
🎯
On the exam. This is heavily tested on the Texas portion. Memorize the five liens, the acreage limits, and the redemption periods (2 years / 180 days / zero).
Encumbrances, Liens & Government Powers (PETE)▾

Encumbrances — anything limiting title

An encumbrance is any claim or restriction that limits the use or transfer of property. It can be financial (a lien) or non-financial (an easement, encroachment, or deed restriction).

Liens
A lien is a money claim against property. Voluntary liens are agreed to (a mortgage). Involuntary liens are imposed by law (tax lien, judgment lien). General liens hit all of a person's property; specific liens attach to one property.

Lien priority generally follows “first recorded, first paid” — except property tax liens, which hold super-priority and are always paid first, no matter when recorded.

Easements
A right to use another's land. An easement appurtenant benefits an adjacent parcel and runs with the land. An easement in gross belongs to a person or company (a utility line) and does not attach to neighboring land. Easements arise by express grant, reservation, implication, necessity, or prescription.
Encroachments & deed restrictions
An encroachment is a physical intrusion (a fence over the line), usually revealed by a survey. Deed restrictions and CC&Rs are private limits enforced by other property owners, not the city.

The four government powers — PETE

PPolice power
EEminent domain
TTaxation
EEscheat
PowerWhat it does
Police powerZoning and regulation for public welfare; no pay unless a regulatory taking
Eminent domainGovernment takes private land for public use with just compensation (process = condemnation)
TaxationAd valorem property tax; creates a super-priority lien
EscheatState takes title when an owner dies with no will and no heirs
💡
Memory trick. PETE — Police power, Eminent domain, Taxation, Escheat. The four sticks government keeps even over private land.
Worked example
The state widens a highway and needs a strip of a homeowner's front yard. It uses eminent domain, pays just compensation, and if the owner refuses, files a condemnation action. This is a public use, so the power applies — but the owner must be paid fairly.
🎯
On the exam. Distinguish eminent domain (takes land, pays) from police power (regulates, usually no pay). Escheat requires BOTH no will AND no heirs.

Agency, Contracts & Financing

Module 2 — The Word for This Module
“A faithful man will abound with blessings.”
Proverbs 28:20
Faithfulness is the thread running through agency, contracts, and financing alike. Learn the duties and the paperwork as the shape that faithfulness takes in a transaction.
Agency Relationships▾

Who works for whom

Agency is created when a principal (client) hires a broker to act on their behalf. The broker is the legal agent. A sales agent works under the broker's license — the sales agent is the broker's agent, and the broker owes the duties to the client.

The fiduciary duties — OLD CAR
An agent owes six fiduciary duties to the client:
OObedience (to lawful instructions)
LLoyalty
DDisclosure (affirmative)
CConfidentiality
AAccounting
RReasonable care
💡
Memory trick. OLD CAR. And remember: Confidentiality NEVER expires — it survives even after the agency relationship ends.

Agency can be express (a written or spoken agreement) or implied (created by conduct). To the client the agent owes fiduciary duties; to third parties the agent still owes honesty and fair dealing.

Listing agreements
The three main employment contracts between seller and broker:
Listing typeWho gets paid
Exclusive right to sellThe listing broker is paid no matter who sells the property
Exclusive agencyBroker paid unless the seller sells it themselves (then no commission)
Open listingOnly the broker who actually produces the buyer is paid
Worked example
A seller signs an exclusive-right-to-sell listing, then finds their own buyer at a barbecue. Does the broker still get paid? Yes — under exclusive right to sell, the broker earns the commission regardless of who finds the buyer.
🎯
On the exam. Confidentiality surviving forever is a classic trap. And know the pay rule for each listing type — especially that exclusive right to sell pays the broker no matter who sells.
Elements of a Valid Contract — COLE▾

What makes a contract enforceable

A valid, enforceable contract needs four elements. Remember them as COLE.

CCompetent parties
OOffer & acceptance
LLegal purpose
EExchange of consideration

A minor's contract is voidable, not void — the minor can choose to enforce or escape it. Offer and acceptance must follow the mirror-image rule: acceptance must match the offer exactly. Any change is a counteroffer, which kills the original offer.

TermMeaning
VoidNo legal effect from the start
VoidableValid until one party chooses to cancel
ExecutedFully performed by both sides
ExecutorySomething still remains to be done
BilateralA promise for a promise
UnilateralA promise in exchange for an act
💡
Memory trick. COLE — Competent, Offer/acceptance, Legal purpose, Exchange of consideration. “Any change to an offer is a counteroffer that kills the original.”
Worked example
A buyer offers $300,000. The seller responds “I accept, but at $310,000.” That is not an acceptance — it changed a term, so it is a counteroffer. The original $300,000 offer is dead and the buyer is now free to walk away or respond.
🎯
On the exam. The mirror-image rule and “counteroffer kills the original” show up almost every exam. A minor's contract is voidable, not void.
Financing Fundamentals▾

How buyers pay for property

Financing questions test the vocabulary of loans and a few key numbers.

Loan-to-value (LTV)
LTV = Loan amount ÷ Appraised value. On a conventional loan, an LTV above 80% requires private mortgage insurance (PMI), because the borrower has less than 20% equity.
Loan typeKey trait
ConventionalNot government-backed; PMI if over 80% LTV
FHAGovernment-insured; requires MIP (mortgage insurance premium)
VAFor veterans; no PMI; charges a funding fee
USDARural properties; up to 100% financing
Points and amortization
One point equals 1% of the loan amount, paid up front, usually to lower the interest rate. Amortization is the gradual payoff of principal and interest over the loan term until the balance reaches zero.
💡
Memory trick. “Over 80% LTV = PMI” on conventional loans. VA has no PMI (funding fee instead); FHA uses MIP.
Worked example
A buyer borrows $250,000 and pays 2 points. Two points = 2% of $250,000 = $5,000 paid at closing. If the appraised value is $312,500, the LTV is $250,000 ÷ $312,500 = 80%, so PMI is not required.
🎯
On the exam. Expect an LTV calculation and a PMI-threshold question (80%), plus “which loan has no PMI” (VA).
Listing Types, Contract Classifications & Leases▾

Pulling the pieces together

This section connects the listing agreements from agency with the lease types you will see in property management.

Property manager = general agent
A property manager is a general agent — authorized to handle a range of ongoing tasks. A listing agent, by contrast, is usually a special agent hired for one transaction.
Lease typeHow rent works
Gross leaseTenant pays fixed rent; landlord pays operating expenses
Net lease (NNN)Tenant pays rent plus taxes, insurance, and maintenance
Percentage leaseRent is a base amount plus a percentage of the tenant's sales
💡
Memory trick. Gross = tenant pays one flat rent. Net (NNN) = tenant also covers taxes, insurance, maintenance. Percentage = common in retail malls, rent rises with sales.
Worked example
A boutique in a mall pays $2,000 base rent plus 5% of monthly sales over $40,000. In a month with $60,000 in sales, the percentage rent is 5% of $20,000 = $1,000, for total rent of $3,000. That is a percentage lease.
🎯
On the exam. Match the lease type to who pays expenses. NNN shifts taxes, insurance, and maintenance to the tenant.

Valuation, Fair Housing & Title

Module 3 — The Word for This Module
“The simple believe every word, but the prudent considers well his steps.”
Proverbs 14:15
The prudent consider their steps. Here you learn to weigh value honestly, to deal justly under fair-housing law, and to trace clean title — discernment applied to real things.
The Three Approaches to Value▾

Value is not the same as price

Market value is what a property should sell for under normal conditions. Market price is what it actually sold for, which may differ. Appraisers estimate value using three approaches.

1. Sales comparison approach
Compares the subject to recently sold similar properties (comps). Golden rule: adjust the COMP to the SUBJECT. If a comp HAS a feature the subject lacks, SUBTRACT from the comp. If the comp LACKS a feature the subject has, ADD to the comp. Best for homes.
💡
Memory trick. “Comp Better, Subtract. Comp lacks, Add.” You never adjust the subject — always the comps.
2. Cost approach
Value = Land value + (Reproduction or replacement cost − Depreciation). Best for new or special-purpose buildings. Three kinds of depreciation: physical, functional, and external. External (economic) obsolescence is incurable because it comes from outside the property.
3. Income approach
Used for income-producing property. The IRV triangle: Income = Rate × Value, so Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate. Always use net operating income (NOI), never gross. A higher cap rate produces a LOWER value.
ApproachBest for
Sales comparisonSingle-family homes
CostNew or special-use buildings (schools, churches)
IncomeRental and commercial property
Worked example
A small apartment building produces $60,000 in net operating income with a market cap rate of 8%. Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate = $60,000 ÷ 0.08 = $750,000. If investors demanded a 10% cap rate, value would drop to $600,000 — higher rate, lower value.
🎯
On the exam. The IRV triangle and “higher cap rate = lower value” are tested often. Remember to adjust the comp, not the subject, and that external obsolescence is incurable.
Fair Housing — FReSH CoRN▾

The protected classes

The federal Fair Housing Act bars discrimination in residential housing based on seven protected classes. Remember them as FReSH CoRN.

FFamilial status
RRace
SSex
HHandicap/disability
CColor
RReligion
NNational origin

Familial status and handicap/disability were added by the 1988 amendments. Race was the earliest protection and is never exempt.

Illegal practices
Steering (guiding buyers toward or away from areas based on a protected class), blockbusting (scaring owners into selling by claiming a group is moving in), and redlining (denying loans or insurance in certain areas).
The Mrs. Murphy exemption
A narrow exemption may apply when ALL THREE are true: the dwelling is owner-occupied with four or fewer units, no broker is used, and no discriminatory advertising is used. Miss any one and the exemption is gone.

Advertising rule: describe the PROPERTY, never the PERSON. An ad that says “perfect for a Christian family” is illegal discriminatory advertising because it signals a preference by religion. Describe bedrooms, not buyers.

💡
Memory trick. FReSH CoRN — the seven protected classes. “Describe the property, never the person.”
Worked example
An agent tells a family, “you'll feel more comfortable in the neighborhood across town where there are more families like yours.” That is steering — directing buyers based on a protected class — and it violates fair housing even if the agent means well.
🎯
On the exam. Race is never exempt. The Mrs. Murphy exemption needs all three conditions. Advertising language about the ideal buyer's religion, family, or origin is illegal.
Deeds, Title & Transfer▾

Title vs. deed

Title is the legal right to own property. A deed is the written document that transfers title from grantor to grantee. You hold title; you sign and deliver a deed.

Elements of a valid deed
A valid deed needs competent grantor, named grantee, consideration, a legal description, words of conveyance (granting clause), and the grantor's signature, then delivery and acceptance. Note: the grantee does not sign the deed.
Deed typeProtection level
General warrantyFullest protection; warrants the entire chain of title
Special warrantyWarrants only the grantor's period of ownership
QuitclaimNo warranties; conveys whatever interest, if any, the grantor has
💡
Memory trick. General = whole chain. Special = my time only. Quitclaim = “I give up whatever I might have, and promise nothing.”
Recording
Recording a deed in the county gives constructive notice to the world. Texas uses a race-notice recording statute: a later buyer who records first and buys in good faith without notice can prevail.
Worked example
A seller conveys a home by general warranty deed. Years later a hidden lien from a prior owner surfaces. Because a general warranty deed covers the entire chain of title, the seller's warranties protect the buyer even against that earlier defect.
🎯
On the exam. Know the three deed types by protection level and that recording gives constructive notice. Texas is a race-notice state.
ADA, Trust Accounts & the Appraisal Process▾

Three exam favorites in one place

ADA vs. Fair Housing
The Americans with Disabilities Act applies to commercial and public accommodations — requiring accessibility. Fair Housing applies to residential dwellings. Do not mix them up.
Trust accounts
Broker escrow/trust money must be kept separate from the broker's own funds. Commingling (mixing client funds with personal funds) is prohibited. Conversion (using client funds for the broker's own use) is a crime. An employing broker can carry vicarious liability for a sales agent's mishandling.
💡
Memory trick. Commingling = mixing (a violation). Conversion = stealing (a crime). Keep client money in its own account, always.
The appraisal process — six steps
Define the problem → determine Scope → Collect data → Analyze (apply the approaches) → Reconcile the values → Report the final opinion. Reconciliation weighs the approaches; it is not a simple average.

Every appraisal rests on highest and best use — the use that is legally permitted, physically possible, financially feasible, and maximally productive.

Worked example
A broker deposits a buyer's earnest money into the broker's operating account “just for a day.” That is commingling and is prohibited even briefly. If the broker then spends it, that becomes conversion — a criminal act.
🎯
On the exam. ADA = commercial; Fair Housing = residential. Commingling vs. conversion is a classic pair. Highest and best use has four tests: legal, physical, feasible, maximally productive.

Worked Examples — Show Your Process▾

Slow is smooth, smooth is fast

The students who pass do not rush. They write down the formula, plug in the numbers, and check the units. Watch three worked problems.

Worked example
Commission split. A house sells for $400,000 at 6%. The listing and selling brokers split 50/50, and each agent keeps 70% of their broker's half. Step 1: total = $400,000 × 0.06 = $24,000. Step 2: each broker gets $12,000. Step 3: each agent gets $12,000 × 0.70 = $8,400.
Worked example
Tax proration. Annual tax is $4,380. The seller owned the home for 90 days into the tax year. Daily = $4,380 ÷ 365 = $12. Seller's share = $12 × 90 = $1,080, charged to the seller at closing.
Worked example
Working backward. A flipper sold a house for $253,000, a 15% profit over cost. Original cost = $253,000 ÷ 1.15 = $220,000. If you multiplied instead, you would get the wrong answer — to find the original, divide.
💡
Memory trick. Write the formula before you touch the calculator. Then plug, solve, and sanity-check the size of the answer.
🎯
On the exam. Multi-step commission problems are common. Do the total first, then the broker split, then the agent split — in that order.
RESPA, TILA, TRID & Closing Calculations▾

The disclosure laws

RESPA
The Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act prohibits kickbacks and required-use referral fees. It governs settlement costs on most residential loans.
TILA / Regulation Z
The Truth in Lending Act requires clear disclosure of the APR and financing terms. It gives a 3-day right of rescission on refinances of a primary residence — but NOT on a purchase loan.
TRID timing
TRID combined the disclosures. The Loan Estimate is due within 3 business days after application. The Closing Disclosure must reach the borrower at least 3 business days before closing.
💡
Memory trick. “Three days after apply (Loan Estimate); three days before close (Closing Disclosure).” Rescission is for refinances, not purchases.

Closing debits and credits

ItemBuyerSeller
Purchase priceDebitCredit
New mortgage loanCredit—
Earnest money already paidCredit—
Seller's existing loan payoff—Debit
Prepaid taxes (seller paid ahead)DebitCredit
Unpaid taxes (seller owes)CreditDebit
Worked example
A buyer's earnest money of $5,000 was paid weeks ago. At closing it appears as a credit to the buyer, because they already put that money toward the purchase. The purchase price itself is a debit to the buyer and a credit to the seller.
🎯
On the exam. The right of rescission applies to refinances, not purchase loans. Memorize the Loan Estimate (3 days after application) and Closing Disclosure (3 days before closing) timing.

Texas TREC, TRELA & Licensing

Module 5 — The Word for This Module
“Where there is no counsel, the people fall; but in the multitude of counselors there is safety.”
Proverbs 11:14
Where there is no counsel, people fall. This module is the counsel of the law itself — TRELA, TREC, and the rules that govern your license. Study them as your safeguard.
TREC, TRELA & Commission Powers▾

Law vs. regulator

TRELA is the Texas Real Estate License Act — the LAW. TREC is the Texas Real Estate Commission — the REGULATOR that enforces the law. The lens for every state answer is public protection.

What TREC CAN do
Investigate complaints, suspend or revoke licenses, impose administrative fines, and require education. TREC's job is to protect the public.
What TREC CANNOT do
TREC cannot send anyone to jail and cannot directly award money damages to a wronged consumer — those come from the courts.
💡
Memory trick. TREC can discipline your license; the courts handle jail and money damages. “Public protection” is the answer to close state questions.

A sales agent must always work under a sponsoring broker, and all compensation flows through that broker. Entity brokerages must have a designated broker.

Worked example
A consumer complains that an agent misrepresented a property. TREC investigates and can suspend the agent's license and fine them — but if the consumer wants money back, they must sue in court. TREC does not hand out damages.
🎯
On the exam. Know the split: TREC = discipline/education/fines; courts = jail and damages. All pay goes through the sponsoring broker.
Recovery Trust Fund — Critical Numbers▾

A safety net funded by license holders

The Real Estate Recovery Trust Fund pays consumers who win a court judgment against a license holder but cannot collect it. Memorize the caps — they are pure recall points.

CapAmount
Maximum per transaction$50,000
Maximum per license holder (lifetime)$100,000
💡
Memory trick. Recovery Fund: $50,000 per transaction, $100,000 per license holder. “Win in court first, then apply.”
How it works
The consumer must first win a court judgment, then apply to the fund when the licensee cannot pay. When the fund pays out, the licensee's license is automatically revoked. The licensee must repay the fund with interest before ever being relicensed.
Worked example
A buyer wins a $65,000 judgment against an agent for fraud, but the agent has no money. The buyer applies to the Recovery Trust Fund. The per-transaction cap is $50,000, so the fund pays $50,000. The agent's license is automatically revoked and cannot be restored until the fund is repaid with interest.
🎯
On the exam. These numbers are almost guaranteed: $50,000 per transaction, $100,000 per license holder. Payment triggers automatic revocation.

Worked example
A bankruptcy trustee, appointed by a court, sells a debtor's property as part of the case. No real estate license is required, because the trustee acts under a court order as a fiduciary — a recognized TRELA exemption.

Texas Homestead Protection (State Review)▾

Homestead lives in both property law and Texas state law

This is the same powerful protection from Module 1, revisited here because it is core Texas state content. If you learned it once, this is your review; if it slipped, lock it in now.

The essentials
Automatic and constitutional creditor protection (no application needed — unlike the tax exemption). Acreage limits: 10 urban, 200 rural family, 100 rural single. Both spouses must sign to sell or encumber.

Texas Intermediary Brokerage▾

Texas does not recognize dual agency

In many states one broker can represent both sides as a “dual agent.” Texas does not use that term. Instead, Texas uses intermediary brokerage.

How intermediary works
A broker may act as an intermediary between a buyer and seller only with written consent from BOTH parties. Written consent is mandatory, not optional. The broker stays neutral and may appoint different license holders to each party. Those appointed license holders CAN advocate for the party they are appointed to.
💡
Memory trick. Texas: no dual agency — intermediary instead. “Written consent from both, broker stays neutral, appointees may advocate.”
Worked example
A broker's two agents each have a client — one the buyer, one the seller — on the same house. With written consent from both parties, the broker becomes an intermediary and appoints each agent to their own client. The appointed agents can advocate; the broker remains neutral.
🎯
On the exam. Two facts win these questions: Texas has no dual agency, and intermediary requires written consent from both parties.
TREC Promulgated Contracts▾

Fill the blanks; don't draft the law

License holders must use TREC-promulgated forms when a form exists for the transaction. Agents fill in the blanks; they do not draft custom contract language, because drafting legal provisions is the unauthorized practice of law.

When something unusual comes up
For special provisions or unusual terms, refer the parties to an attorney. Agents may add factual business details in the blanks, but attorneys draft new legal provisions.
💡
Memory trick. “Agents fill blanks; attorneys draft provisions.” Using a promulgated form is required when one exists.

Common addenda handle financing, repairs, HOA information, and option periods. Reaching for an addendum is often the correct move rather than rewriting the contract.

Worked example
A seller wants an unusual clause tying the sale to a complex tax exchange. The agent should not write that clause — that would be unauthorized practice of law. The correct step is to refer the seller to an attorney to draft the provision.
🎯
On the exam. If a question asks whether an agent can write custom legal language, the answer is no — refer to an attorney. Promulgated forms are mandatory when available.
Texas Special Topics — DTPA, Foreclosure & Landlord-Tenant▾

Three Texas-specific areas

DTPA
The Deceptive Trade Practices Act protects consumers from deceptive acts. Knowing violations can bring treble (triple) damages, so honesty is not just ethics — it is expensive to break.
Texas foreclosure
Texas commonly uses non-judicial foreclosure through a deed of trust with a power-of-sale clause. Foreclosure sales occur on the first Tuesday of the month. There is no right of redemption after a mortgage foreclosure (unlike a tax sale).
Landlord-tenant
No self-help evictions — a landlord cannot change locks or remove belongings without following the legal process. Security deposits must be returned within the statutory window after move-out.
💡
Memory trick. DTPA = triple damages for knowing violations. Texas foreclosure = “first Tuesday, no redemption” for mortgages. No self-help evictions.
Worked example
A landlord, frustrated with a late-paying tenant, changes the locks and sets the tenant's furniture on the curb. That is an illegal self-help eviction under Texas law — the landlord must go through the courts.
🎯
On the exam. Remember: DTPA treble damages, Texas non-judicial foreclosure on the first Tuesday with no mortgage redemption, and no self-help evictions.
Community Property, Wills, Recording & Ethics▾

Rounding out Texas law

Community property, deeper
Property acquired during marriage is community property. Income from separate property becomes community property. Spouses can sign a written partition agreement to divide community property, and a premarital agreement can set terms before marriage.
Wills and estates
A testator makes a will; a gift of real property by will is a devise, and the recipient is a devisee. Dying without a will is intestate, and property passes by Texas intestate succession. Joint tenancy with survivorship passes outside probate.
Recording and ethics
Texas uses a race-notice recording statute. All advertising must include the broker's name, and social media follows the same rules as print. Agents may be compensated only through their sponsoring broker, may not split fees with unlicensed persons, and may not engage in the unauthorized practice of law.
💡
Memory trick. “Broker's name in every ad — including social media.” No fee-splitting with unlicensed people. Pay flows only through your broker.
Worked example
An agent posts a listing on Instagram without the broker's name. That violates Texas advertising rules — social media is held to the same standard as newspaper ads, and the sponsoring broker's name must appear.
🎯
On the exam. Ethics recall points: broker's name in all ads (including social), pay only through the broker, no splitting with unlicensed persons, and no unauthorized practice of law.
