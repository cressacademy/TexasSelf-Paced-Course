Property, Ownership & Texas Homestead

Module 1 — The Word for This Module
“The earth is the Lord's, and the fulness thereof.”
Psalm 24:1
You are learning to steward land and property well. Approach every ownership question as a caretaker of something entrusted, not merely a test item.
Real Property, Personal Property & Fixtures▾
Land, real estate, and real property

Start with three layers. Land is the surface, everything beneath it to the center of the earth, and the air above it. Real estate is land plus anything permanently attached by nature or by people. Real property is real estate plus the legal bundle of rights that comes with ownership. Personal property (also called chattel) is everything movable that is not permanently attached.

Fixtures and the MARIA test

A fixture is personal property that has become real property by being permanently attached. Whether an item is a fixture is decided by five factors, remembered as MARIA:

MMethod of attachment
AAdaptability to the property
RRelationship of the parties
IIntention of the person
AAgreement between the parties
💡
Of all five factors, Agreement is the tie-breaker. If the parties put it in writing, that controls — regardless of how the item is attached.
Trade fixtures
Items a tenant attaches to conduct business (shelving, a barber's chair) remain the tenant's personal property and may be removed before the lease ends. If left behind, they become the landlord's by accession.
🎯
On the exam, a chandelier, built-in bookcase, or planted shrub is real property; a potted plant, area rug, or free-standing refrigerator is personal property. Emblements — annual crops — stay personal property of the tenant farmer.
The Bundle of Rights & Estates in Land▾
The bundle of rights

Ownership is a bundle of rights: the right to possess, use (enjoy), exclude others, encumber, and dispose (sell, gift, will). A useful memory word is DEEP-C? Simpler: think of the rights to possess, control, enjoy, exclude, and dispose. An owner can sell or give away individual rights (for example, granting an easement) while keeping the rest.

Freehold estates

A freehold estate is ownership of indefinite duration. The greatest is fee simple absolute — full ownership with no conditions. A fee simple defeasible ownership can be lost if a stated condition is violated. A life estate lasts for someone's lifetime; when it ends, the property passes to the remainderman or reverts to the grantor (reversion).

Leasehold estates

A leasehold is a tenant's right to possess for a period. The four types: estate for years (fixed start and end, no notice needed to end), periodic estate (renews automatically, e.g., month-to-month, needs notice), estate at will (indefinite, either party ends anytime), and estate at sufferance (tenant holds over wrongfully).

EstateDurationNotice to end?
Estate for yearsFixed termNo — ends automatically
PeriodicRenews each periodYes
At willIndefiniteYes (either party)
At sufferanceHoldover — wrongfulN/A
🎯
Fee simple = inheritance and indefinite duration. A life estate is NOT inheritable by the life tenant's heirs — it ends at the measuring life.
Forms of Co-Ownership & the Four Unities▾
Ownership in severalty vs. co-ownership

Ownership by one person or entity alone is ownership in severalty (severed from all others). When two or more own together, the form of co-ownership determines their rights and what happens at death.

The four unities — TTIP

Joint tenancy requires four unities. Remember them as TTIP:

TTime — acquired at the same moment
TTitle — same deed/document
IInterest — equal shares
PPossession — equal right to the whole

Joint tenancy carries the right of survivorship: when one owner dies, their share passes automatically to the surviving joint tenants, not to heirs.

The main forms
FormSurvivorship?Notes
Tenancy in commonNoUnequal shares allowed; each share is inheritable
Joint tenancyYesFour unities (TTIP); equal shares
Tenancy by the entiretyYesBetween spouses only; requires marriage as a fifth unity
💡
Tenancy in Common = Can will your share (no survivorship). Joint tenancy = survivorship; the last survivor owns it all.
🎯
A tenant in common may sell or will their share freely. Selling a joint-tenancy share breaks that unity and converts the buyer's interest to a tenancy in common.
Government Powers (PETE) & Encumbrances▾
The four government powers — PETE

No ownership is absolute; government retains four powers over private land, remembered as PETE:

PPolice power — zoning, codes, regulation
EEminent domain — take for public use, pay just compensation
TTaxation — real estate taxes
EEscheat — property reverts to the state when an owner dies with no heirs and no will
Eminent domain vs. condemnation
Eminent domain is the government's power to take private property for public use with just compensation. Condemnation is the process used to exercise it. Inverse condemnation is when an owner forces the government to buy land it has effectively taken.
Encumbrances

An encumbrance is any claim, lien, or restriction that affects title or use but does not prevent transfer. Two families:

TypeExamplesAffects
Liens (money claims)Mortgage, tax lien, mechanic's lien, judgmentValue / title
Non-money encumbrancesEasements, encroachments, deed restrictions, licenseUse / physical

An easement appurtenant benefits an adjoining parcel (the dominant tenement) and burdens another (the servient tenement); it runs with the land. An easement in gross benefits a person or company (a utility line) with no dominant parcel. An encroachment is an unauthorized physical intrusion, such as a fence over the property line.

💡
A general lien attaches to all of a person's property (judgment, estate tax); a specific lien attaches to one identified property (mortgage, mechanic's lien, property tax).
🎯
Priority of liens is generally 'first to record, first in right' — EXCEPT property tax and special assessment liens, which take priority over all others regardless of recording date.
Texas Homestead Protection▾

Automatic, constitutional, and strong

Texas homestead protection shields a family's primary residence from most creditors. It is automatic and constitutional — you do not apply for the creditor protection. Do not confuse it with the homestead tax exemption, which does require an application.

Acreage limits
Urban homestead: up to 10 acres. Rural homestead for a family: up to 200 acres. Rural homestead for a single adult: up to 100 acres.
💡
Memory trick. Homestead acreage: 10 urban, 200 rural family, 100 rural single. “Ten in town, two hundred for a family in the country, one hundred single.”
The liens that CAN still reach a homestead
Homestead protection is strong but not absolute. Five debts can still attach: (1) purchase-money mortgage, (2) property taxes (super-priority), (3) mechanic's/construction liens properly signed, (4) HOA assessments, and (5) home-equity loans.
Lien on homesteadRedemption period
Property tax sale2 years
HOA foreclosure180 days
Mortgage foreclosureNone (zero)

Homestead rights end only by sale, death, or abandonment — and cannot be waived by contract. Both spouses must sign to sell or encumber the homestead: “one to buy, two to sell.”

Worked example
A family owns a home on 8 acres inside city limits with a mortgage. A credit-card company wins a judgment and tries to force a sale. Homestead protection blocks it — the 8 acres is within the 10-acre urban limit, and a general judgment creditor is not one of the five liens that can reach a homestead.
🎯
On the exam. This is heavily tested on the Texas portion. Memorize the five liens, the acreage limits, and the redemption periods (2 years / 180 days / zero).
Community Property, Wills, Recording & Ethics▾

Rounding out Texas law

Community property, deeper
Property acquired during marriage is community property. Income from separate property becomes community property. Spouses can sign a written partition agreement to divide community property, and a premarital agreement can set terms before marriage.
Wills and estates
A testator makes a will; a gift of real property by will is a devise, and the recipient is a devisee. Dying without a will is intestate, and property passes by Texas intestate succession. Joint tenancy with survivorship passes outside probate.
Recording and ethics
Texas uses a race-notice recording statute. All advertising must include the broker's name, and social media follows the same rules as print. Agents may be compensated only through their sponsoring broker, may not split fees with unlicensed persons, and may not engage in the unauthorized practice of law.
💡
Memory trick. “Broker's name in every ad — including social media.” No fee-splitting with unlicensed people. Pay flows only through your broker.
Worked example
An agent posts a listing on Instagram without the broker's name. That violates Texas advertising rules — social media is held to the same standard as newspaper ads, and the sponsoring broker's name must appear.
🎯
On the exam. Ethics recall points: broker's name in all ads (including social), pay only through the broker, no splitting with unlicensed persons, and no unauthorized practice of law.

Agency, Fiduciary Duties & Texas Intermediary

Module 2 — The Word for This Module
“He that is faithful in that which is least is faithful also in much.”
Luke 16:10
Agency is a trust relationship. Faithfulness in small duties — a returned call, a disclosed fact — is the same character the exam is testing in principle.
Agency Relationships & Types▾
The parties

In an agency relationship, the principal (client) hires an agent to act on their behalf. The agent owes the principal fiduciary duties. A customer is a party the agent works with but not for — owed honesty and fairness, but not fiduciary loyalty. A subagent is an agent of the agent.

Scope of authority
TypeAuthority
Special agentOne specific task (most brokers listing one home)
General agentA range of activities (a property manager)
Universal agentBroad power to act (power of attorney)
Whom the agent represents

A seller's (listing) agent represents the seller; a buyer's agent represents the buyer. Dual agency is representing both parties in the same transaction — legal only with the informed written consent of both, and it limits the duties the agent can fully perform. Designated agency assigns different agents in the same brokerage to each side.

🎯
A customer is owed honesty and disclosure of material facts, but NOT confidentiality or loyalty. Undisclosed dual agency is illegal in every jurisdiction.
Fiduciary Duties — OLD CAR▾
The duties an agent owes the principal

An agent owes the client six fiduciary duties, remembered as OLD CAR:

OObedience — follow lawful instructions
LLoyalty — put the client's interest first
DDisclosure — reveal all material facts
CConfidentiality — protect the client's secrets
AAccounting — account for all money and documents
RReasonable care & diligence
Obedience has a limit
The agent must obey lawful instructions only. An instruction to conceal a defect or to discriminate must be refused — obedience never overrides the law or duty to third parties.
Confidentiality survives
The duty of confidentiality continues even after the agency relationship ends. An agent may not later reveal that a former seller-client would have accepted less.
💡
Loyalty means no undisclosed profit and no self-dealing. If the agent buys the client's property or has any personal interest, it must be disclosed in writing.
🎯
Duties to the CLIENT: full OLD CAR. Duties to CUSTOMERS/third parties: honesty, fair dealing, and disclosure of known material defects. Do not confuse the two lists on the exam.
Creating & Terminating Agency; Disclosure▾
How agency is created

Agency is usually created by an express agreement (a written listing or buyer-representation agreement). It can also arise by implied conduct, by ratification (approving an act after the fact), or by estoppel (a principal lets others reasonably believe agency exists). Agency does not require that a commission be paid — compensation and representation are separate questions.

How agency ends

Agency ends by completion of the purpose, expiration of the term, mutual agreement, revocation or renunciation, or by operation of law (death, incapacity, or bankruptcy of either party, or destruction of the property).

Disclosure of agency

Agents must disclose whom they represent, usually in writing and at first substantive contact. Failing to disclose can create undisclosed dual agency or an accidental agency the broker never intended.

💡
Death or destruction ends agency automatically — no notice required. Compare that to a lease's estate for years, which survives the death of either party.
🎯
A listing agreement is a personal-services contract between seller and BROKER, not the individual salesperson. If the salesperson leaves the firm, the listing stays with the broker.
Antitrust & Professional Practice▾
Antitrust — the four violations

Federal antitrust law (the Sherman Act) forbids agreements that restrain competition. In real estate, four violations recur:

ViolationWhat it is
Price fixingCompetitors agreeing to set commission rates
Group boycottingCompetitors agreeing to refuse to deal with a broker
Market allocationDividing territories or client types among competitors
Tie-in agreementsForcing a second purchase as a condition of the first
Commissions are always negotiable
There is no 'standard' or 'going' rate. Any statement implying a set commission across competing brokerages risks a price-fixing claim. Each broker sets rates independently.
💡
If two competing brokers even discuss setting the same rate, that conversation alone can be price fixing — no signed agreement is required.
🎯
Penalties under the Sherman Act are severe: criminal fines and imprisonment for individuals, plus large corporate fines and triple (treble) damages in civil suits.
Texas Intermediary Brokerage▾

Texas does not recognize dual agency

In many states one broker can represent both sides as a “dual agent.” Texas does not use that term. Instead, Texas uses intermediary brokerage.

How intermediary works
A broker may act as an intermediary between a buyer and seller only with written consent from BOTH parties. Written consent is mandatory, not optional. The broker stays neutral and may appoint different license holders to each party. Those appointed license holders CAN advocate for the party they are appointed to.
💡
Memory trick. Texas: no dual agency — intermediary instead. “Written consent from both, broker stays neutral, appointees may advocate.”
Worked example
A broker's two agents each have a client — one the buyer, one the seller — on the same house. With written consent from both parties, the broker becomes an intermediary and appoints each agent to their own client. The appointed agents can advocate; the broker remains neutral.
🎯
On the exam. Two facts win these questions: Texas has no dual agency, and intermediary requires written consent from both parties.

Contracts & Texas Promulgated Forms

Module 3 — The Word for This Module
“Let your communication be, Yea, yea; Nay, nay.”
Matthew 5:37
A contract is a promise made plain. Study it as the discipline of saying exactly what you mean and meaning what you sign.
Essential Elements & Classifications▾
The elements of a valid contract — COLE

Every valid contract needs four essentials, remembered as COLE:

CConsideration — something of value exchanged
OOffer & acceptance — a meeting of the minds
LLegal purpose — a lawful objective
E(Legally competent) parties with capacity & consent

Contracts for real estate must also be in writing to be enforceable, under the Statute of Frauds. A genuine offer and acceptance requires mutual assent free of fraud, duress, or misrepresentation.

Valid, void, voidable, unenforceable
TermMeaning
ValidMeets all elements; fully enforceable
VoidNo legal effect from the start (illegal purpose)
VoidableValid until one party chooses to cancel (minor, fraud, duress)
UnenforceableValid but a court will not enforce (not in writing)
Bilateral vs. unilateral; express vs. implied

A bilateral contract is a promise for a promise (a sales contract). A unilateral contract is a promise for an act (an option; a reward). An express contract is stated in words; an implied contract is shown by conduct.

💡
A contract with a minor is voidable by the minor — not void. The minor may enforce it or walk away; the adult is bound.
🎯
Real estate sales contracts must be written and signed to satisfy the Statute of Frauds. An oral agreement to sell land is unenforceable.
Sales Contracts & Contingencies▾
The purchase agreement

The sales (purchase) contract binds buyer and seller. Until all conditions are met and title transfers, the buyer holds equitable title — an interest in the property — while the seller keeps legal title. Earnest money is a good-faith deposit held in a trust/escrow account, applied to the purchase at closing.

Contingencies

A contingency is a condition that must be satisfied or the contract can be canceled without penalty. Common ones: financing, inspection, appraisal, and sale of the buyer's current home. If a contingency fails within its deadline, the buyer usually recovers the earnest money.

Offer, counteroffer, acceptance

A counteroffer rejects and replaces the original offer — the original is dead and cannot later be accepted. An offer may be revoked any time before acceptance is communicated. Once accepted and communicated, a binding contract exists.

💡
Equitable title = the buyer's interest after signing but before closing. It is what lets a buyer sue for specific performance if the seller backs out.
🎯
A counteroffer terminates the original offer. If the seller counters and the buyer walks, the seller cannot go back and accept the buyer's first offer.
Listing & Buyer-Representation Agreements▾
Types of listings
ListingWho can earn the commission?
Exclusive right to sellThe listing broker earns it no matter who sells
Exclusive agencyThe broker earns it unless the OWNER sells it themselves
Open listingOnly the broker who procures the buyer earns it; owner may sell alone
Net listings are discouraged or illegal
In a net listing the broker keeps everything above a set price. Because it invites the broker to profit at the seller's expense, it is prohibited or heavily restricted in most places.
Buyer-representation agreements

These mirror listings from the buyer's side, ranging from exclusive to open. They establish the agency relationship and how the buyer's agent is paid.

Procuring cause

The broker who is the procuring cause — the one whose efforts actually led to the ready, willing, and able buyer — earns the commission in an open or disputed situation.

💡
Exclusive RIGHT to sell = broker paid no matter who finds the buyer. Exclusive AGENCY = the owner is the one exception who can sell without paying.
🎯
A commission is earned when the broker produces a ready, willing, and able buyer who meets the seller's terms — even if the seller then refuses to close.
Breach, Remedies, Assignment & Discharge▾
Discharge of contracts

A contract ends (is discharged) by performance, mutual agreement, novation (substituting a new contract or party), assignment (transferring rights to another), or breach.

Assignment vs. novation

An assignment transfers a party's rights to a new party, but the original party may remain liable. A novation replaces a party or contract entirely, releasing the original party from liability.

Remedies for breach
RemedyWhat it does
Specific performanceCourt orders the deal to be completed as agreed
Compensatory damagesMoney to cover actual losses
Liquidated damagesA pre-agreed amount (often the earnest money) kept on breach
RescissionCancel the contract and return parties to their prior positions
💡
Because every parcel of land is considered unique, a buyer can often demand specific performance — money alone may not substitute for the exact property.
🎯
Liquidated damages are agreed in advance (commonly the earnest money as the seller's sole remedy). Do not confuse them with compensatory damages, which are proven after the fact.
TREC Promulgated Contracts▾

Fill the blanks; don't draft the law

License holders must use TREC-promulgated forms when a form exists for the transaction. Agents fill in the blanks; they do not draft custom contract language, because drafting legal provisions is the unauthorized practice of law.

When something unusual comes up
For special provisions or unusual terms, refer the parties to an attorney. Agents may add factual business details in the blanks, but attorneys draft new legal provisions.
💡
Memory trick. “Agents fill blanks; attorneys draft provisions.” Using a promulgated form is required when one exists.

Common addenda handle financing, repairs, HOA information, and option periods. Reaching for an addendum is often the correct move rather than rewriting the contract.

Worked example
A seller wants an unusual clause tying the sale to a complex tax exchange. The agent should not write that clause — that would be unauthorized practice of law. The correct step is to refer the seller to an attorney to draft the provision.
🎯
On the exam. If a question asks whether an agent can write custom legal language, the answer is no — refer to an attorney. Promulgated forms are mandatory when available.

Financing & Mortgages

Module 4 — The Word for This Module
“The rich ruleth over the poor, and the borrower is servant to the lender.”
Proverbs 22:7
Understanding debt with clear eyes is wisdom. Learn financing so you can guide clients honestly through the biggest obligation most will ever sign.
Mortgage Instruments & Clauses▾
The note and the security

Two documents work together. The promissory note is the borrower's promise to repay — the actual debt. The mortgage (or deed of trust) is the security instrument that pledges the property as collateral. If the borrower defaults, the security instrument allows foreclosure.

Mortgage vs. deed of trust
MortgageDeed of trust
PartiesBorrower (mortgagor) & lender (mortgagee)Borrower (trustor), lender (beneficiary) & trustee
ForeclosureUsually judicialOften non-judicial (power of sale)
Key clauses

An acceleration clause lets the lender demand the entire balance on default. A due-on-sale (alienation) clause requires payoff if the property is sold, preventing loan assumption without approval. A prepayment clause governs early payoff penalties. A defeasance clause requires the lender to release the lien once the loan is paid.

💡
Note = the promise to pay (the debt). Mortgage/deed of trust = the pledge of the property (the security). The note follows the borrower; the lien follows the land.
🎯
A due-on-sale clause blocks a buyer from simply assuming the loan; the lender must consent. An acceleration clause is what makes foreclosure of the whole balance possible.
Loan Types & Programs▾
Conventional vs. government-backed

A conventional loan is not insured or guaranteed by the government. If the down payment is under 20% (loan-to-value above 80%), the lender usually requires private mortgage insurance (PMI), which can be removed as equity builds.

ProgramBackingSignature feature
FHAInsured by the Federal Housing AdministrationLow down payment (as little as 3.5%); MIP required
VAGuaranteed by the Dept. of Veterans Affairs0% down for eligible veterans; funding fee
USDAGuaranteed by the Dept. of AgricultureRural areas; low/no down payment
Amortized vs. other structures

A fully amortized loan pays principal and interest so the balance reaches zero at the end. A term (straight) loan pays interest only with principal due at the end (a balloon). An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) has a rate that changes with an index. A package loan includes personal property; a blanket loan covers several parcels with a partial release clause.

💡
FHA insures (MIP) and VA guarantees — the government does not lend the money itself; approved lenders do. VA loans can reach 100% financing.
🎯
PMI (conventional) is different from MIP (FHA). PMI can be canceled as equity grows; FHA MIP often lasts the life of the loan depending on the down payment.
Financing Concepts & the Market▾
Primary vs. secondary market

The primary market is where borrowers get loans from lenders (banks, credit unions, mortgage companies). The secondary market is where those loans are bought and sold, replenishing lenders' funds. Key players: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac buy loans; Ginnie Mae backs government-insured loans.

The Federal Reserve

The Fed influences the cost and supply of money through the discount rate, reserve requirements, and open-market operations. Tightening money tends to raise rates and slow lending; loosening does the opposite. The Fed does not set mortgage rates directly.

Points, LTV, and equity

A discount point equals 1% of the loan amount, paid up front to lower the interest rate (buying down the rate). Loan-to-value (LTV) is loan ÷ value; a lower LTV means more borrower equity and less lender risk.

💡
Fannie & Freddie = the secondary market that keeps money flowing back to lenders. The Fed = the referee of money supply, not the source of your mortgage.
🎯
One point = 1% of the LOAN amount, not the price. Points paid to lower the rate are 'buying down' the loan.
Federal Lending Laws▾
Truth in Lending Act (TILA) & Regulation Z

TILA, implemented by Regulation Z, requires lenders to disclose the true cost of credit, including the annual percentage rate (APR) and finance charges, so borrowers can compare loans. It governs certain advertising: if an ad states a specific credit term (a rate or payment), it must disclose the other required terms (a 'trigger term' rule). It also gives a right of rescission on certain refinances of a primary residence.

RESPA

The Real Estate Settlement Procedures Act covers federally related mortgage loans. It requires a Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure, prohibits kickbacks and unearned fees for referrals, and limits escrow amounts.

ECOA

The Equal Credit Opportunity Act prohibits discrimination in lending based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, or receipt of public assistance.

💡
TILA/Reg Z = disclose the COST of credit (APR). RESPA = govern the SETTLEMENT process and ban kickbacks. ECOA = ban discrimination in CREDIT decisions.
🎯
A vague ad ('great rates!') has no trigger terms and needs no extra disclosure. Stating a specific figure (like '5% APR' or '$900/month') triggers full Reg Z disclosure.

Valuation, Appraisal & Market Analysis

Module 5 — The Word for This Module
“A just weight and balance are the Lord's.”
Proverbs 16:11
Valuation is the discipline of telling the truth about worth. Weigh honestly, and your numbers will serve people rather than mislead them.
Principles & Characteristics of Value▾
The four characteristics of value — DUST

For anything to have value in the market, four elements must be present, remembered as DUST:

DDemand — desire plus purchasing power
UUtility — usefulness for a purpose
SScarcity — limited supply
TTransferability — ability to convey title
Key principles
PrincipleMeaning
Highest & best useThe legal, physical, and financial use that gives the greatest value
SubstitutionA buyer pays no more than the cost of an equally desirable substitute
ConformityValue is maximized when a property conforms to its surroundings
Progression / RegressionA modest home gains value near better homes; a fine home loses value near lesser ones
Supply & demandValue rises when demand exceeds supply
Types of value

Market value is the most probable price a willing buyer and seller would agree to under normal conditions. It differs from market price (what actually sold) and cost (dollars to build). Assessed value is for taxation; insured value is for coverage.

💡
Substitution is the backbone of the sales-comparison approach: buyers won't pay more than a comparable alternative costs.
🎯
Market value is an OPINION of the most probable price under normal terms — not the same as the price a specific property actually sold for (market price).
The Three Approaches to Value▾
Sales comparison approach

The appraiser compares the subject to recently sold comparables and adjusts for differences. The rule: adjust the comparable, never the subject. If a comp is superior, subtract; if inferior, add. This approach is best for single-family homes and relies on the principle of substitution.

Cost approach

Value = land value + (cost to rebuild the improvements − depreciation). Best for new or special-purpose properties (schools, churches) that rarely sell.

Income approach

Used for income-producing property. The core formula ties income, rate, and value:

Worked Example
Value = Net Operating Income ÷ Capitalization Rate. If a building earns $60,000 NOI and the market cap rate is 8%, value = $60,000 ÷ 0.08 = $750,000. A quicker screen for small residential rentals is the Gross Rent Multiplier (GRM) = price ÷ gross rent.
💡
CBS: Cost for new/special-purpose, By-comparison (sales) for homes, Stream of income for rentals.
🎯
Adjust the COMPARABLE, not the subject. Comp better than the subject → subtract; comp worse → add. Reversing this is the most common exam trap.
The Appraisal Process, Depreciation & CMA▾
Depreciation — three kinds
TypeCauseCurable?
Physical deteriorationWear and tear, ageOften curable
Functional obsolescenceOutdated design or featuresSometimes curable
External (economic) obsolescenceOff-site factors (highway, declining area)Incurable — outside the property
External obsolescence is always incurable
Because the cause lies outside the property line — a new landfill next door, a downturn in the local economy — the owner cannot fix it. That distinguishes it from physical and functional problems.
Appraisal vs. CMA vs. BPO

An appraisal is an independent, licensed opinion of value. A comparative market analysis (CMA) is prepared by a licensee to help price a listing — it is not an appraisal. A broker price opinion (BPO) is similar. Reconciliation is the appraiser's weighing of the approaches into a final opinion — never a simple average.

💡
Depreciation for appraisal = loss in value from ANY cause. Depreciation for taxes is a separate accounting write-off; don't mix the two on the exam.
🎯
Only physical and functional problems inside the property can be curable. Anything caused by factors OUTSIDE the property (external/economic obsolescence) is incurable.

Title, Fair Housing & Texas Disclosures

Module 6 — The Word for This Module
“In righteousness shalt thou judge thy neighbour.”
Leviticus 19:15
Fair dealing is not merely the law here — it is the heart of it. Learn these rules as the shape justice takes in a transaction.
Deeds & Transfer of Title▾
What a valid deed requires

A deed transfers title from the grantor to the grantee. Requirements generally include: a competent grantor, a named grantee, consideration, a granting clause (words of conveyance), an accurate legal description, and the grantor's signature and delivery and acceptance. The grantee need not sign.

Types of deeds — by warranty
DeedProtection to grantee
General warrantyGreatest — grantor defends title against all claims, ever
Special warrantyGrantor warrants only against claims during their ownership
Bargain and saleImplies ownership but no warranties
QuitclaimLeast — conveys only whatever interest the grantor has, if any
Title & recording

Recording in the public records gives constructive notice to the world of one's interest. Actual notice is what a person truly knows. A clear chain of title shows ownership passing without gaps. Adverse possession can transfer title to someone who possesses land openly, notoriously, continuously, and hostilely for the statutory period.

💡
General warranty deed = the buyer's best protection. Quitclaim = the weakest, often used to clear a cloud on title, not to sell.
🎯
A deed needs the GRANTOR's signature, not the grantee's. Title passes upon delivery AND acceptance — not at signing.
Title Insurance, Closing & Prorations▾
Title insurance

A title search and abstract reveal the history of ownership and any defects. Title insurance protects against losses from covered title defects. An owner's policy protects the buyer; a lender's (mortgagee) policy protects the lender for the loan amount.

Closing / settlement

At closing, funds and documents change hands. Costs are split by custom and contract. Prorations divide ongoing items (taxes, interest, HOA dues) between buyer and seller as of the closing date. The party who used the item pays for their share.

Worked Example
Annual taxes of $3,650 prorated to the seller through day 100 of the year: $3,650 ÷ 365 = $10/day × 100 days = $1,000 charged to the seller (a credit to the buyer if the seller has not yet paid).
💡
Debit = a charge; Credit = money in your favor. The buyer is credited what the seller owes but hasn't paid; the seller is credited what they prepaid.
🎯
A lender's title policy protects the LENDER, not the buyer. Buyers need a separate owner's policy for their own protection.
Federal Fair Housing — FReSH CoRN▾
The protected classes

The federal Fair Housing Act of 1968 (expanded in 1988) prohibits discrimination in housing based on seven protected classes, remembered as FReSH CoRN:

FFamilial status (children under 18, pregnancy)
RReligion
SSex
HHandicap (disability)
CColor
RRace
NNational origin

Race was the first protected class (Civil Rights Act of 1866) and can never be exempted. The 1988 amendments added familial status and handicap.

Prohibited practices
PracticeWhat it is
SteeringDirecting buyers toward or away from areas based on a protected class
BlockbustingInducing panic selling by claiming a protected group is moving in
RedliningRefusing loans/insurance in certain areas based on protected class
Advertising
Ads may not state a preference or limitation based on a protected class. Saying a home is 'perfect for a Christian family' is illegal because it expresses a religious preference; likewise 'no children' violates familial status. Describe the property, never the desired people.
🎯
Disability rules require allowing reasonable modifications (tenant pays) and reasonable accommodations (like a service animal, even with a no-pets policy). 'Adult communities' (55+) are a narrow exemption to familial status only.
Mandated Disclosures & Practice▾
Lead-based paint

Federal law requires disclosure of known lead-based paint hazards for housing built before 1978. Sellers/landlords must give the EPA pamphlet, disclose known hazards, and offer buyers a 10-day period to test.

Environmental awareness

Agents should recognize (not remediate) hazards: asbestos, radon (a gas from soil), lead, underground storage tanks, mold, and CERCLA/Superfund liability for contamination. Wetlands and flood zones affect use and financing.

Material facts & stigmatized property

Agents must disclose known material defects that affect value or safety. Facts about a prior occupant's illness or death are often not required (and may be protected), but a licensee must answer honestly and never actively conceal a defect.

Trust accounts & advertising

Client funds must be kept in a separate trust/escrow account — never mixed with the broker's money (commingling is prohibited). Advertising must be truthful and, in most jurisdictions, identify the brokerage.

💡
Before 1978 = lead-based paint disclosure + 10-day test window. Radon comes from the ground; asbestos was used in old insulation. Know the source, not the remedy.
🎯
Commingling (mixing client funds with the broker's) and conversion (using client funds) are serious violations. Keep earnest money in the trust account, not the operating account.
Texas Special Topics — DTPA, Foreclosure & Landlord-Tenant▾

Three Texas-specific areas

DTPA
The Deceptive Trade Practices Act protects consumers from deceptive acts. Knowing violations can bring treble (triple) damages, so honesty is not just ethics — it is expensive to break.
Texas foreclosure
Texas commonly uses non-judicial foreclosure through a deed of trust with a power-of-sale clause. Foreclosure sales occur on the first Tuesday of the month. There is no right of redemption after a mortgage foreclosure (unlike a tax sale).
Landlord-tenant
No self-help evictions — a landlord cannot change locks or remove belongings without following the legal process. Security deposits must be returned within the statutory window after move-out.
💡
Memory trick. DTPA = triple damages for knowing violations. Texas foreclosure = “first Tuesday, no redemption” for mortgages. No self-help evictions.
Worked example
A landlord, frustrated with a late-paying tenant, changes the locks and sets the tenant's furniture on the curb. That is an illegal self-help eviction under Texas law — the landlord must go through the courts.
🎯
On the exam. Remember: DTPA treble damages, Texas non-judicial foreclosure on the first Tuesday with no mortgage redemption, and no self-help evictions.

Worked Examples — See the Process▾
Commission split
Worked Example
A $320,000 home sells at 6%. The listing broker and buyer's broker split 50/50, and the listing agent keeps 60% of their broker's half. Total = $320,000 × 0.06 = $19,200. Listing side = $9,600. Listing agent = $9,600 × 0.60 = $5,760.
Profit on a sale
Worked Example
An investor buys at $150,000, spends $20,000, and sells at $204,000. Cost basis = $170,000. Profit = $34,000. Profit % = $34,000 ÷ $170,000 = 20%.
Proration
Worked Example
Seller owes taxes of $4,380/year and closes on day 90 (having paid nothing). Daily = $4,380 ÷ 365 = $12. Seller's share = $12 × 90 = $1,080 (debit seller / credit buyer).
Net to seller
Worked Example
A seller wants $188,000 net after a 6% commission (no other costs). Sale price = $188,000 ÷ (1 − 0.06) = $188,000 ÷ 0.94 = $200,000.
💡
For 'net to seller' problems, DIVIDE by (1 − rate). Do NOT just add the commission percentage to the net — that gives the wrong number.
🎯
Read the base carefully: commission and points are on different numbers (sale price vs. loan). Mixing the bases is the top math error on the exam.

TREC, TRELA & Commission Powers▾

Law vs. regulator

TRELA is the Texas Real Estate License Act — the LAW. TREC is the Texas Real Estate Commission — the REGULATOR that enforces the law. The lens for every state answer is public protection.

What TREC CAN do
Investigate complaints, suspend or revoke licenses, impose administrative fines, and require education. TREC's job is to protect the public.
What TREC CANNOT do
TREC cannot send anyone to jail and cannot directly award money damages to a wronged consumer — those come from the courts.
💡
Memory trick. TREC can discipline your license; the courts handle jail and money damages. “Public protection” is the answer to close state questions.

A sales agent must always work under a sponsoring broker, and all compensation flows through that broker. Entity brokerages must have a designated broker.

Worked example
A consumer complains that an agent misrepresented a property. TREC investigates and can suspend the agent's license and fine them — but if the consumer wants money back, they must sue in court. TREC does not hand out damages.
🎯
On the exam. Know the split: TREC = discipline/education/fines; courts = jail and damages. All pay goes through the sponsoring broker.
Licensing Requirements — Know Every Number▾

The numbers TREC will test

RequirementNumber
Pre-license education180 classroom hours
Passing score (each portion)70% on national AND 70% on state
Background checkRequired
RenewalContinuing education required
💡
Memory trick. 180 pre-license hours. 70% to pass — and you must pass BOTH the national and state portions separately.

You must pass each portion on its own. A great national score cannot rescue a failing state score, and vice versa. A background check (fingerprints) is required, and continuing education keeps the license active at renewal.

Worked example
A candidate scores 85% national but 65% state. Did they pass? No — each portion needs 70% independently. They keep the passing national portion (within the allowed window) and retake only the state portion.
🎯
On the exam. 180 hours and 70%/70% are frequent recall items. Remember the two portions are scored separately.
Recovery Trust Fund — Critical Numbers▾

A safety net funded by license holders

The Real Estate Recovery Trust Fund pays consumers who win a court judgment against a license holder but cannot collect it. Memorize the caps — they are pure recall points.

CapAmount
Maximum per transaction$50,000
Maximum per license holder (lifetime)$100,000
💡
Memory trick. Recovery Fund: $50,000 per transaction, $100,000 per license holder. “Win in court first, then apply.”
How it works
The consumer must first win a court judgment, then apply to the fund when the licensee cannot pay. When the fund pays out, the licensee's license is automatically revoked. The licensee must repay the fund with interest before ever being relicensed.
Worked example
A buyer wins a $65,000 judgment against an agent for fraud, but the agent has no money. The buyer applies to the Recovery Trust Fund. The per-transaction cap is $50,000, so the fund pays $50,000. The agent's license is automatically revoked and cannot be restored until the fund is repaid with interest.
🎯
On the exam. These numbers are almost guaranteed: $50,000 per transaction, $100,000 per license holder. Payment triggers automatic revocation.
License Exemptions Under TRELA▾

Who does NOT need a license

Some people can perform real-estate-like acts without a license because they are acting in another recognized capacity.

Exempt partyWhy
Property ownersActing on their own behalf with their own property
AttorneysActing within the practice of law
Court-appointed fiduciariesExecutors, receivers, bankruptcy trustees acting under court order
Government employeesActing in their official capacity
💡
Memory trick. Owners for themselves, attorneys in practice, court-appointed fiduciaries under order, and government employees on the job — no license needed.
Worked example
A bankruptcy trustee, appointed by a court, sells a debtor's property as part of the case. No real estate license is required, because the trustee acts under a court order as a fiduciary — a recognized TRELA exemption.
🎯
On the exam. Exemptions require acting in the recognized capacity. An owner selling their own property is exempt; an owner selling a neighbor's property for a fee is not.
